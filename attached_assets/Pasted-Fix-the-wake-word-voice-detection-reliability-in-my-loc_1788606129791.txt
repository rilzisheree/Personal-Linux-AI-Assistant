Fix the **wake-word voice detection reliability** in my local AI assistant **Lura**.

The wake word is unreliable: I can speak loudly and clearly, but Lura often fails to detect it. Sometimes it works, but frequently it doesn't.

## GOAL

Make wake-word detection significantly more reliable without causing excessive false activations.

Inspect the entire pipeline:

```text
Microphone
↓
Audio capture
↓
Audio buffer/chunks
↓
Preprocessing
↓
Wake-word detector
↓
Confidence score
↓
Threshold
↓
Debounce/cooldown
↓
Wake-word activation
↓
Voice assistant
```

## INVESTIGATE FIRST

Do not immediately change the threshold.

Inspect the existing implementation and determine whether the problem is caused by:

* incorrect microphone/device selection
* wrong sample rate
* wrong number of channels
* audio format mismatch
* incorrect chunk size
* chunks being dropped
* insufficient audio buffering
* gaps between audio chunks
* wake-word model receiving incomplete windows
* incorrect resampling
* excessive noise suppression
* excessive filtering
* incorrect normalization
* lack of gain/AGC
* VAD incorrectly rejecting speech
* wake-word confidence threshold being too high
* confidence scores being interpreted incorrectly
* sliding-window/overlap problems
* detector cooldown being too long
* detector state not resetting correctly
* microphone initialization problems
* detector stopping while idle
* audio stream becoming stale after a command
* another process interfering with the microphone

## TEST DIFFERENT CONDITIONS

Test wake-word detection under:

1. normal speaking volume
2. loud speaking
3. quiet speaking
4. microphone close to mouth
5. microphone farther away
6. background noise
7. silence
8. after Lura has been idle for a long time
9. immediately after Lura finishes a command
10. repeated wake-word attempts

Determine whether confidence scores are consistently below the current threshold.

If the threshold is the issue, make it configurable and tune it based on actual observed confidence values rather than guessing.

## AUDIO BUFFERING

Verify that the detector receives continuous audio.

Check for:

```text
dropped chunks
buffer underruns
buffer overruns
gaps
incorrect window sizes
insufficient overlap
```

If the wake-word model requires a specific sample rate/window size, ensure the audio reaching it matches those requirements exactly.

## FALSE POSITIVE PROTECTION

Do not solve missed detections by simply making the threshold extremely low.

Test common speech and background noise to ensure the detector doesn't constantly activate.

Use appropriate:

* confidence threshold
* debounce
* cooldown
* consecutive-frame confirmation

if supported by the existing detector.
